03 / HOW IT HAPPENED

A fake invitation led to device compromise

The approach began as a podcast invitation about Mintropolis. A fraudulent page impersonating StreamYard was presented as the meeting platform. When the installation appeared to fail, the supposed organizers instructed me to run a Terminal-based installer on my Mac and grant administrator access.

The sender then asked for my wallet address, saying they would send an NFT gift. Around that time I unlocked MetaMask in Chrome. Unauthorized NFT sales, transfers and cryptocurrency withdrawals followed. I did not knowingly authorize or voluntarily arrange the transactions that removed the listed assets.

My X account was also taken over. The identity behind the invitation is unresolved; an account or displayed name used in the approach does not establish the identity of the attacker.

What the local device review found

The retained technical summary describes a root-level persistent component named “cloudsync” and a fake ChatGPT application. Static analysis identified clipboard-monitoring and browser/wallet-data collection capabilities. These findings describe capabilities, not proof of exactly what was extracted or how the blockchain transactions were signed.

Known malicious components were disabled or removed according to the incident records. Invitation messages, screenshots, transaction records, malware hashes and technical notes were retained privately. This page does not certify the device as clean or publish malware installation instructions.

06 / FBI · IC3 & OPENSEA

FBI/IC3 complaint summary

The original saved IC3 submission is dated 31 October 2026; the saved update is dated 5 November 2026. The submissions describe unauthorized NFT sales/transfers, cryptocurrency withdrawals and a social-account takeover. The update expressly declares all 109 itemized NFTs stolen and requests that the expanded schedule be associated with the original complaint.

The support records describe OpenSea requesting an updated report naming the additional 81 Robinhood assets and confirming the theft declaration. Filing records and a marketplace restriction are different things: neither confirms an FBI investigative finding, recovery of the NFTs or the identity of an offender.

Later buyers, recipients and current holders are not accused of involvement merely because they received or hold an NFT.
